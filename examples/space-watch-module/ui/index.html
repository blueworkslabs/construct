Help and data sources

Tap a dot on the dome or an entry in the list. Directions use fists at arm’s length: one fist is about 10° above the horizon. The dome is drawn like a compass held flat: north up, east right. Bright dots with a track are sunlit against a dark sky and should be visible; faint rings are in Earth’s shadow or in daylight. Rewind shows the last five minutes for something you just saw.

Visible passes lists what should be visible over the next 12 hours; tap one to preview it on the dome. Trains are fresh batches of Starlink, Qianfan or Guowang satellites that still fly close together, like a line of lights, for days to weeks after launch. Their early orbits are approximate, so the line can be ahead of or behind the drawing and stretch over several fists.

Follow turns the dome with your phone: hold it flat, top pointing where you face, and the green wedge shows your view. Raise the phone like a camera and it becomes a pointing view: the back of the phone is the aim, and the words and arrow say where to move it until the selected satellite sits in the ring. There is no camera image; the view is drawn. It needs “Allow reading compass and tilt” in Construct’s Module access. Headings are corrected to true north with the World Magnetic Model. If that correction is unavailable, the sky stays north-up and pointing guidance pauses. Phone compasses are rough (often ±10–20°) and metal nearby confuses them; wave the phone in a figure 8 when asked. Compass readings are used only while Follow is on and are never stored or sent.

The list covers CelesTrak’s ~150 brightest objects, not every satellite. Brightness varies and tumbling rocket stages flicker. Positions come from published orbits and can be off by seconds, more when the orbit data is old.

Your location stays on this phone: Space Watch downloads the whole bright-object list and works out positions itself. The start choice and red mode are remembered; coordinates never are. Orbit data is cached for a few hours. Details can ask Wikipedia about the selected object by name, only when you tap Read on Wikipedia.

Orbits and catalog: CelesTrak (celestrak.org). Stars: Yale Bright Star Catalog. Positions: satellite.js and Astronomy Engine (MIT). Magnetic declination: World Magnetic Model WMM2025 (NOAA/BGS). Descriptions: Wikipedia, CC BY-SA 4.0.
